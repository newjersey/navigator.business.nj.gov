heading-1: Get Help with Permits and Compliance
main-text-1: Getting government permits can be hard, even for real estate
  professionals. The New Jersey Business Action Center
  ([NJBAC](https://www.nj.gov/state/bac/)) can help. NJBAC gets answers from
  government agencies and points you to the right officials. It can also set up
  meetings and follow up with these offices for you.
heading-2: Understand Building Permits and Inspections
main-text-2: If you're planning construction, renovations, or repairs to your
  space, you'll probably need a building permit. You usually need a permit for
  building, structural, electrical, HVAC, and plumbing projects. Permits make
  sure that work follows building and zoning rules. They also help keep your
  team and customers safe.
heading-3: Determine if You Need a Building Permit
main-text-3: >-
  Building permits are issued by Municipal Building andConstruction Departments.
  Projects that may require a permit include:


  * New buildings or structures

  * Additions to a building

  * Structural alterations to a building or structure

  * Interior modifications and changes to a floor plan

  * Changes in building use and/or occupancy

  * Parking layout changes

  * Use-of-land projects, such as recycling deposit centers, open-air storage, and outdoor temporary events

  * Signs and sign structures

  * Fences, retaining walls, and shoring plans
heading-4: Learn How to Get a Building Permit
main-text-4: >-
  Each municipality may have a slightly different process, but most of the forms
  used to get a Construction Permit can be used in any municipality. The
  [Uniform Construction Code
  forms](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/constructionpermitforms.shtml)
  should be filed with the local Building Department.


  Contact the [Business Action Center](https://www.nj.gov/state/bac/) if you need help getting a building permit.
heading-5: Sign Up for an Inspection
main-text-5: >-
  Once your building permit is approved, you can start work. You must also set
  up the required inspections. Inspectors check the work before it is finished
  or covered up. This makes sure the project meets the permit rules.


  Depending on the type and size of your project, you may need several inspections. Check with your local Building or Construction Department for a list of required inspections.


  Sometimes a municipality sends all or part of a building project to another office for review and inspection. This may be the State Department of Community Affairs or a nearby municipality. A municipality may do this if it has no staff with the right license for the job. It may also do this if a staff member has a conflict of interest and must step aside.
heading-6: Make Your Site ADA Compliant
main-text-6: >-
  The American with Disabilities Act (ADA) and New Jersey rules protect people
  with disabilities. People with disabilities have the right to use businesses
  and other public places. Your business should be easy for everyone to use, and
  give all customers the same access. 


  The ADA is a federal civil rights law. It protects people with disabilities or impairments by making sure public places can accommodate different needs. These include stores, hotels, buses and trains, and other businesses open to the public.


  [Federal ADA regulations](https://www.ada.gov/) require new construction for public accommodations to comply with the [2010 Americans with Disabilities Act Standards](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/). Public accommodations must also remove architectural barriers when possible, even when they are not planning construction. This can include existing barriers at entrances, aisles, bathrooms, and service counters that predated the ADA or the current business owner's occupancy.


  Make sure to learn about ADA requirements to help ensure accessibility for your customers and reduce the risk of potential lawsuits.
heading-7: Meet State Requirements for Air Pollution When Doing Construction
main-text-7: >-
  The Department of Environmental Protection's Bureau of Stationary Sources is
  responsible for permitting stationary sources of air pollution, such as
  factories and power plants. The Bureau regulates both existing and new
  facilities to help ensure they do not adversely affect air quality in New
  Jersey.


  Some businesses may require air permits for their operations.

heading-8: Meet State Requirements for Land Use and Water Protection
main-text-8: >-
  The Department of Environmental Protection regulates New Jersey's land and
  natural resources to protect drinking water, waterways, and wildlife.
  Depending on your project, regulations may include the Freshwater Wetlands
  Protection Act, Flood Hazard Area Control Act, Wetlands Act of 1970, Coastal
  Area Facility Review Act, Waterfront Development Law, Tidelands Act, NJ Water
  Pollution Control Act, and the Highlands Water Protection and Planning Act.


  The [NJBAC](https://www.nj.gov/state/bac/) can assist you in navigating these requirements and maintaining compliance.

heading-10: Obtain Miscellaneous Traffic Permits for Access to State Roadways
main-text-10: >-
  Property owners seeking access to state roadways and transportation
  infrastructure must submit applications to the New Jersey Department of
  Transportation (NJDOT). Access issues may affect:


  * Persons seeking new access to State highways

  * Persons seeking to modify existing access to State highways

  * Persons seeking to renovate existing access to State highways

  * Telecommunication carriers seeking access for tower sites


  ## Obtain a Certificate of Need Program for Facilities that Meet Community Needs


  A Certificate of Need is a regulatory process governing the construction, relocation, or renovation of certain healthcare facilities. The program helps ensure that new facilities and expansions of existing facilities meet community needs and helps avoid investment in duplicate facilities.
heading-11: Ensure Your Site Meets Fire Safety Requirements
main-text-11: >-
  After completing site construction or renovation and passing all required
  building inspections, register your business with the state Division of Fire
  Safety or your Local Enforcing Agency (LEA), as applicable, before beginning
  operations.


  LEAs, such as fire prevention bureaus, fire departments, and fire districts, enforce state and local fire safety regulations. Contact your local LEA to determine whether you need to register with the State or the LEA and how to complete the registration. Ask how often your business must be inspected after opening so you can remain compliant.
